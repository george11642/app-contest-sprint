## Email details

- To: `itevents@unm.edu`
- Technical subject: `App Contest Technical Submission – <Team or App Name>` (en dash, as on the site)
- Deck subject: `App Contest Presentation Submission – <Team or App Name>`

## Required technical attachments

1. Short bio of the developer or team
2. 3 screenshots of the application (may be used on score cards or in promotion)
3. Purpose, audience, functions, pathways to satisfaction
4. Proposed technical aspects, with an architecture diagram (Visio, Lucidchart, Excalidraw or similar; a legible screenshot is fine)
5. Security
6. A link to the prototype, viewable by anyone with the link

Items 1 to 5 are "PDF or Word Doc Attachment only, do not send in a link". The prototype is the only link allowed.

## Scoring

**Technical, 100 points, scored by the IT technical team:** 25 each for Purpose/Audience/Functions/Pathway, Proposed Technical Aspects, Security, Prototype. The prototype must be high fidelity and "clearly demonstrating user flows and application primary use case functionality".

**Extra technical points, maximum 10:** kickoff Sep 17 (+5), mentoring session Oct 8 or 20 (+5, once). The rules say these "often make a difference". The first cut uses technical score plus extra points.

**Presentation, 100 points, 25 each:**
1. Definition and analysis of the problem, and fit with the mission
2. Originality and coherence in design and experience
3. Functionality and features
4. Professionalism in communication and presentation

**Final score** is technical plus extra plus presentation.

The bio and screenshots are required but appear in no scoring row. Do them well enough, fast. Never trade prototype or security time for them.

## Mission

The 2026 contest "focuses on developing a plan and a pitch". The submission is "your proposal and road map for further coding and development". A plan with honest status beats a claim that everything is finished.

## Gotchas

| Gotcha | Consequence | Source |
|---|---|---|
| A coded app alone is not accepted | Submit a prototype and pitch deck whatever else you built | FAQ |
| Entry must be "a novel idea for a currently unpublished application" | No App Store or Play release before the contest ends. TestFlight is fine. A public production site is a risk; ask itevents@unm.edu | RULES |
| An app presented in an earlier year cannot be resubmitted | Check during the scan | FAQ |
| Team, name or contact changes only until the first email deadline | Email itevents@unm.edu before Nov 6 | FAQ |
| Eligibility: current UNM or CNM student with 3+ fall credit hours, or a current-year CNM Deep Dive cohort, registered by the deadline | Check every teammate | RULES |
| No outside computers or devices on the Rainforest system | The demo is slides plus embedded video on their machine | FAQ, SUBMIT |
| One unofficial transcript per teammate, hard copy, at practice. "Do NOT email these transcripts!!" | A human prints them | SUBMIT, FAQ |
| 8 minutes, then 4 minutes of Q&A, strictly timed | Rehearse with a clock | RULES, SUBMIT |
| Presentation judges "will not have seen any of your materials" | The deck must stand alone and define every term | RULES |
| Rights stay with entrants, but the contest may use images, names and screenshots | Screenshots carry no real personal data | RULES |

## Judges (roles on the JUDGES page)

An external relations manager at a science museum, a CNM Deep Dive program director, a game and web developer who is a past winner, a UNM IT applications programmer, a UNM data privacy and compliance specialist, a Java and Android instructor, a staff engineer in streaming, and an Adobe Education customer manager.

Expect privacy, Android and architecture questions in Q&A. The technical round is scored by the IT team, so the security document may reach a security reader there too.
